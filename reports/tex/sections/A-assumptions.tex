\section{Choices the paper leaves open}
\label{app:assumptions}

Each entry is marked \code{[ASSUMPTION]} at the place in the code where it enters.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.2em,style=nextline,itemsep=2pt]
\item[Optimiser schedule (\cref{sec:kwsa}).]  Step sizes $a_n = a_0/(n+1)^{0.602}$,
difference step $c_n = c_0/(n+1)^{0.101}$, geometric cooling; one iteration is a coordinate
sweep.  Bit-count: $a_0 = 20$, $c_0 = 0.2$, $T_0 = 0.02$, cooling $0.7$, 20 sweeps.  Flappy:
$a_0 = 0.4$, $c_0 = 0.25$, $T_0 = 0.05$, cooling $0.9$, 25 sweeps after 300 random restarts.
Iris: $a_0 = 2$, decay exponent $0.3$, $c_0 = 0.1$, 40 sweeps.  ECG: 40 sweeps per restart (the
\code{ecg\_table1.py} default; the library default is 100), 8 restarts, best by training MSE.
\item[Iris ambiguous codes (\cref{sec:iris}).]  Under the default policy, samples sharing a
12-bit code are merged and their labels averaged.  The author's own preprocessing removed
``binary patterns which were assigned to two classes'' (e-mail, 2026-08-21); our
\code{ambiguous="drop"} does that on both splits after encoding, so the test accuracy under
it is conditional on the pattern being unambiguous (seed 0: $97.6\,\%$ on the 82 surviving test
samples against $95.0\,\%$ on all 100); both numbers are reported.  Which split the author
filtered, and whether before or after the k-means encoding, is not stated.
\item[Iterative baseline (\cref{sec:bitcount}).]  The paper describes neither the sample-wise
scheme nor its ``iteration''.  Ours: one \emph{elementary} Kiefer--Wolfowitz step per iteration
--- one coordinate, one randomly chosen input, four objective evaluations (two probes, the
step, the re-scored incumbent) --- on the squared error
of that input's activation, with only the KW step offered to the Metropolis rule
(\code{probe\_candidates = False}), one coordinate per iteration (\code{n\_coords = 1}) and a
fixed schedule $a = 2$, $c = 0.05$, $T = 0$ (\code{ITERATIVE\_CONFIG}); $10^5$ iterations with
the per-input $\KL$ monitored every $1\,000$.  Identifying the paper's plotted iteration with
this elementary step is itself the assumption that makes its $\approx 120$ iterations and our
$10^5$ steps comparable; the authors' hint to use ``1000 times more iterations'' is consistent
with it.
\item[Angle encoding (\cref{eq:encoding}).]  $\theta = 2\arcsin\sqrt s$, pinned by a printed
angle in \cite{snn}; $\theta = \pi s$ tested as an ablation.
\item[KL regularisation.]  The model probability is floored at $10^{-12}$ inside the logarithm.
\item[Iris (\cref{sec:iris}).]  Stratified one-third training split; flowers sharing a code are
merged and their labels averaged; the KL is normalised over all 4\,096 input strings (restricting
it to the dataset's codes lets the network exploit interference between examples and reach a
training loss of $10^{-5}$ while reading out at chance); label weight $\propto(\ell+1)^2$ read
from Fig.~8; labels predicted by nearest rounding of the read-out weight.
\item[Flappy (\cref{sec:flappy}).]  Our own clone: 288$\times$512 screen, flap impulse $-7$, gap
120, pipe spacing 180, gravity 1 (the classic constants are unplayable by a memoryless two-feature
policy); reward 0.01 per frame plus 1 per pipe, tracking term $|{\rm height}-{\rm gap}|$ with
weight 1; 5 training episodes of 1\,500 frames, 20 held-out seeds 5--24, 5\,000-frame cap (110
pipes); decisions cached on a $65\times65$ grid of observations.
\item[ECG (\cref{sec:ecg}).]  Three signals: a synthetic McSharry-form signal tuned to Fig.~13
with Gaussian noise $\sigma = 0.02$; MIT-BIH record 100; and the paper's own recording, sent by
the first author on 21 August 2026 and loaded by \code{-{}-source mailed}, of which we use the
first $1\,800$ samples --- the Fig.~13 window --- because the full $7\,700$ exceed the compute
ceiling.  On that recording nothing about the noise is ours: it arrives already noisy and clipped
to $[0,1]$, with measured $\sigma = 0.035$ and input PSNR $27.0$~dB.  What remains assumed for
all three signals: mirror-padded edges; first half train, second half test (the paper gives no
split); 8 optimiser restarts per cell with the best taken by training MSE (the paper gives no
restart or selection protocol); PSNR with $R = $ range of the clean signal; SSIM both
whole-signal (Eq.~(8)) and in a 7-sample sliding window.
\item[Noise model (\cref{sec:devices}).]  Depolarising at the vendor's average gate error,
relaxation during gates and as-soon-as-possible idling, readout confusion; median values where
vendors publish per-qubit tables; no crosstalk (no vendor publishes a usable number); where a
vendor publishes nothing for a channel it is modelled as error-free and flagged.  Quantinuum's
idle error is charged as $(1-m)^{n_{\rm qubits}\cdot{\rm depth}}$ from its memory error per
depth-1 time; IQM's and Rigetti's unpublished one-qubit gate durations are set to 40\,ns.
\item[Fidelity estimate (\cref{eq:fidelity}).]  First-order product of per-gate survival
probabilities; readout factor $(1-(p_{01}+p_{10})/2)^n$.
\item[Costs (\cref{sec:cost}).]  IBM \$1.60 per QPU second (2022 launch figure, corroborated by
a secondary \$96/min report) plus 30\,s per-job overhead; Quantinuum \$12.5 per credit (Azure
Standard plan divided by its allotment); Braket and Azure per-shot prices as retrieved on
20 August 2026; Quantinuum's credit formula with $N_m = 2\times$ qubits.
\item[State preparation (\cref{sec:recommendation}).]  Dense counts $2^{n+1}-2n$ \cite{sbm} and
$\approx\tfrac{23}{24}2^n$ \cite{plesch}; sparse \emph{leading term} $k\cdot n$ of an $O(k n)$
construction \cite{gleinig} with $k$ the $12$--$14$ distinct training codes actually prepared
(constant unknown).
\item[Compilation.]  Lattice machines are compiled onto a synthetic heavy-hex or square patch of
the circuit's size with our greedy router; Google's and Rigetti's gate sets are rebased through a
generic fallback.  Idle relaxation is charged over each qubit's schedule gaps only.
\item[Shot-noise programme (\cref{sec:shots}).]  Margin penalty $w\cdot 0.01\cdot\max(0,1-\delta/m)$
per frame with $m=0.1$; sequential test with $\alpha=\beta=0.05$, blocks of 20 shots; common
random numbers by maximal coupling to the first probe of each coordinate.
\end{description}
